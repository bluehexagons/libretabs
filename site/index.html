Your first practice session

  1. Start with the ready exercise

    Read Quick start, choose Start practicing, then press Play to listen once. No account or music file is needed. For another tune, open Menu → Songs and try Twinkle, Twinkle Little Star or Mary Had a Little Lamb.

  2. Read the guitar tabs

    Tablature, or tabs, uses six lines for the guitar strings. The top line is string 1, the thinnest string. A number tells you which fret to use, not which finger. Press just behind that metal fret and pluck the string; 0 means pluck without pressing. Read left to right. The tabs assume standard guitar tuning: E–A–D–G–B–E from thickest to thinnest. Menu → Help explains the sheet music and symbols.

  3. Listen, then play along

    Open Menu → Playback → Speed presets and try 50%: half speed, with the same pitches. A metronome clicks with the beat; a count-in gives you clicks to get ready before the music. Choose Stop, then Play to start again. Outlined tab numbers show sounding notes. With menus closed, Space plays or pauses.

  4. Repeat a small section

    A measure is a group of beats between vertical lines. Open Menu → Loop, set From 1 and Through 2, and choose Turn loop on. Close the menu and press Play to repeat both measures. Choose Turn loop off to continue through the song.

Read the full user guide for tab and rhythm basics, every practice feature, keyboard shortcuts and troubleshooting.

Use your own music

Open Menu → Songs → Open MIDI file and choose a .mid or .midi file. MIDI contains notes and timing, not recorded sound: MP3s, PDFs and photos cannot be opened as songs. The prototype accepts Standard MIDI formats 0 and 1, up to 256 KiB, with further limits on song length and note count.

If the file has several parts, use Choose the part to practice to pick a melody or instrument. In Menu → Volume & parts, use Mute my part when you want to supply that part on your guitar, and choose which other parts to hear.

Under Songs → About this arrangement, choose Basic tab, Strum with a pick or Fingerpick. Read the warnings: the strumming and fingerpicking tabs may omit notes from crowded chords. The sheet music and sound still include those notes. These are generated suggestions, not reviewed guitar transcriptions.

Make it comfortable

The menu offers text size, light/dark appearance, reduced motion, control placement and handed layout. In Score view, choose scrolling or pages. Turning a page by hand stops automatic page following without moving the sound; enable Follow playback to resume it. Fewer Music lines make each line taller; tighter Note spacing fits more notes across. Add or reorder tabs, sheet music and piano rows as needed.

Theater shows more music on desktops, tablets and larger screens. Use Show controls or F10 if playback controls hide. Fullscreen is separate; Theater does not connect to a TV itself.

Keyboard reference notes default to Z X C V B N M ,. An alternate A-row layout is available in keyboard settings. These notes are for reference; they do not record or grade your playing.

Print or capture

Print your music prepares a self-contained printable HTML file with tabs, staff, or both. Open the saved file in a browser to print or save as PDF.

Capture & overlay provides a score-only view for streaming or instructional video. Choose Enter capture view after setting the background and music layout. Use separate software to record or stream it. F8 toggles it; Escape or a tap returns to the player.

If something gets in the way

I can’t hear the notes

Click or tap Play to allow browser audio. Check the device and browser-tab volume, then Menu → Volume & parts: raise Instrument volume and turn off Mute my part. If playback is stuck at 0% speed, raise it and press Play. Try a built-in exercise to check whether the problem also happens without an imported song.

My MIDI file does not sound or look like the original

MIDI describes notes and timing, not a recording or a finished guitar score. Choose a pitched part; drum-only files cannot produce a guitar part. The prototype simplifies notation and proposes guitar placement. Check its warnings and try another part or a simpler file.

How do I use a download offline?

Extract the entire Windows or Linux ZIP before opening the application. No Godot installation is needed. The web ZIP is for hosting; opening its index.html file directly will not run the player. Browser offline use requires a completed first download and retained site storage.

A new version isn’t appearing

Finish your session before reloading: imported songs must be reopened afterward. Reconnect to the internet and reload the player. If it still shows an old version, report the URL and version. Clearing site data removes saved preferences and offline files, so avoid it as a first step.

The player says my browser is missing a required feature

Try the compatibility player linked near the top of this guide. It does not use browser threads and may work on devices where the main player cannot start, though audio response and performance can be less consistent.

What to expect—and what to report

This is an early technical prototype. Guided lessons are incomplete. Guitar arrangements and simplified notation still need musical review. It does not listen to your guitar or assess your performance. Mobile and browser compatibility remain under evaluation.

Imported MIDI stays on your device and is session-only. Preferences are stored locally when storage is available. Offline browser use requires a completed first download; cleared browser storage requires reconnection. Native downloads are an alternative.

Report a bug or confusing interaction with your version, device, browser and steps to reproduce it. Use original or redistributable music examples; don’t attach private or copyrighted songs.
